## Background

Hendrik Willem van Loon was born in the Netherlands and moved to the United States as a young man. He was a historian, journalist and artist. The Story of Mankind (1921) is a history of the whole world written for young readers, and it won the first Newbery Medal, an important American prize for children's books, in 1922.

This excerpt is the opening of the first chapter. Van Loon begins history long before humans appear, with the formation of the earth and the first living things. Of course, science has learned a lot since 1921, but his basic picture is still close to what scientists believe. In the original book, the paragraph about two lines refers to a small drawing, in which a long line shows the history of animal life and a tiny line shows the history of humans. As you read, notice how the writer makes huge, difficult ideas easy and exciting.

## Excerpt

We live under the shadow of a gigantic question mark.

Who are we?

Where do we come from?

Whither are we bound?

Slowly, but with persistent courage, we have been pushing this question mark further and further towards that distant line, beyond the horizon, where we hope to find our answer.

We have not gone very far.

We still know very little but we have reached the point where (with a fair degree of accuracy) we can guess at many things.

In this chapter I shall tell you how (according to our best belief) the stage was set for the first appearance of man.

If we represent the time during which it has been possible for animal life to exist upon our planet by a line of this length, then the tiny line just below indicates the age during which man (or a creature more or less resembling man) has lived upon this earth.

Man was the last to come but the first to use his brain for the purpose of conquering the forces of nature. That is the reason why we are going to study him, rather than cats or dogs or horses or any of the other animals, who, all in their own way, have a very interesting historical development behind them.

In the beginning, the planet upon which we live was (as far as we now know) a large ball of flaming matter, a tiny cloud of smoke in the endless ocean of space. Gradually, in the course of millions of years, the surface burned itself out, and was covered with a thin layer of rocks. Upon these lifeless rocks the rain descended in endless torrents, wearing out the hard granite and carrying the dust to the valleys that lay hidden between the high cliffs of the steaming earth.

Finally the hour came when the sun broke through the clouds and saw how this little planet was covered with a few small puddles which were to develop into the mighty oceans of the eastern and western hemispheres.

Then one day the great wonder happened. What had been dead, gave birth to life.

The first living cell floated upon the waters of the sea.

For millions of years it drifted aimlessly with the currents. But during all that time it was developing certain habits that it might survive more easily upon the inhospitable earth. Some of these cells were happiest in the dark depths of the lakes and the pools. They took root in the slimy sediments which had been carried down from the tops of the hills and they became plants. Others preferred to move about and they grew strange jointed legs, like scorpions and began to crawl along the bottom of the sea amidst the plants and the pale green things that looked like jelly-fishes. Still others (covered with scales) depended upon a swimming motion to go from place to place in their search for food, and gradually they populated the ocean with myriads of fishes.

## Appreciation

Van Loon begins with a powerful image: we "live under the shadow of a gigantic question mark". Then he asks three short questions: Who are we? Where do we come from? Whither are we bound? These are the biggest questions humans ask, and he puts them on their own lines. The layout makes them feel important, like a poem.

He is also honest about the limits of knowledge: "We have not gone very far." He adds little comments in brackets, "(with a fair degree of accuracy)" and "(according to our best belief)". These show a good scientific attitude: he tells us what we know and what we only guess.

The writing uses many images from the theatre. The chapter is called "Setting the Stage", and he says he will explain how "the stage was set for the first appearance of man". History becomes a great play, and the earth is its stage.

The description of the young earth is dramatic and visual. The planet is "a large ball of flaming matter". Rain falls "in endless torrents". Then the sun breaks through and sees "a few small puddles which were to develop into the mighty oceans". The contrast between "puddles" and "mighty oceans" shows how much time has passed.

The most beautiful moment is very short: "What had been dead, gave birth to life." Van Loon's sentences are simple, but he chooses strong verbs and bright images. That is why young readers have enjoyed his history for a hundred years.

## Sentences to imitate

> We live under the shadow of a gigantic question mark.
A strong metaphor to open an essay about a big question.

> We still know very little but we have reached the point where (with a fair degree of accuracy) we can guess at many things.
"Reach the point where…" describes progress.

> Then one day the great wonder happened. What had been dead, gave birth to life.
A short, dramatic pair of sentences for a turning point.

> Man was the last to come but the first to use his brain for the purpose of conquering the forces of nature.
"The last to… but the first to…" is a neat contrast.

## Notes

- whither | Old word meaning "to where". | 往何处
- persistent | Continuing firmly, not giving up. | 坚持不懈的
- torrents | Large amounts of fast-flowing water. | 急流；倾盆
- granite | A very hard grey rock. | 花岗岩
- hemispheres | Halves of the earth (eastern and western, northern and southern). | 半球
- inhospitable | Difficult to live in. | 不适宜居住的
- sediments | Sand, mud or stones that settle at the bottom of water. | 沉积物
- myriads | Very large numbers. | 无数
- amidst | Among. | 在……之中
