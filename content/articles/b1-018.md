When the sun goes down in many Asian cities, a different life begins. Roads fill with small lights, and the smell of hot food floats through the air. This is the night market, and for many visitors, it is the best part of the trip.

I visited one last year with a friend who lives in the city. We arrived at seven o'clock, and it was already full of people. On both sides of a long street, there were hundreds of small shops. Some sold clothes, bags, and toys. Others sold phone cases, shoes, and cheap jewellery. People were walking slowly, looking at everything, and asking for a lower price. My friend told me, "Never pay the first price. Smile, and say you will think about it."

But the best thing was the food. There were so many kinds that I did not know where to start. One woman was cooking noodles in a big, hot pan, and the smoke rose high above her head. A man was selling chicken on sticks, still hot from the fire. Another stall had sweet pancakes with banana and chocolate. There were also fruit drinks, fried fish, and soup with small balls of meat. Every dish cost only a few dollars.

We ate standing up, or sitting on small plastic chairs with strangers. Nobody minded. When my friend ordered something I did not know, I was a little afraid, but it was delicious. Many people at the next table laughed and showed us how to eat it the right way.

At ten o'clock, we were full and tired, with bags in our hands and sweet sticky fingers. The market was still busy, and a band was playing on a small stage.

I will never forget that evening. A night market is more than a place to shop and eat. It is a place where people meet, talk, and enjoy the city together, long after the day has finished.
