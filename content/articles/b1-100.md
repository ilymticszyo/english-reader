The great stone buildings of Egypt are some of the most famous in the world. They stand in the desert near the city of Giza. They are very old. The largest was built about four and a half thousand years ago, and it still stands today.

These buildings were graves for kings. The Egyptians believed that after death, a king would begin a new life. So the grave had to be a safe and grand home. Inside, they placed food, gold, and other things that the king might need.

The biggest one belonged to a king called Khufu. It was the tallest building in the world for more than 3,700 years. It is made of more than two million blocks of stone. Each block is very heavy, as much as a small car.

How did people build it? Experts do not know every detail. They think that thousands of workers took part. In the past, many people believed that slaves built these places. But scientists have found the towns where the workers lived. These workers were paid and were given bread, beer, and meat. They worked in teams, and each team had a name.

The workers cut the stone from nearby places. They moved the blocks on wooden frames, and they probably poured water on the sand in front to make it easier to pull them. Then they lifted the stones into place with long slopes made of earth.

Today, millions of visitors come to see them. Many questions remain, and scientists are still making new discoveries. This is part of what makes them so interesting.
