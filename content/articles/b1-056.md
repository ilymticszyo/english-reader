We write many emails every week, but not all of them are good. A clear email saves time. A confusing one can cause problems. Here are some simple tips.

First, write a clear subject line. The subject is the first thing that people see. "Question about Friday's meeting" is much better than "Hello" or nothing at all.

Second, begin in a polite way. If you know the person, you can write "Dear Anna" or "Hi Anna". If you do not know the person, use "Dear Mr Brown" or "Dear Ms Green". At the end, write "Best wishes" or "Kind regards" and then your name.

Third, put the most important thing first. Many people read emails quickly, often on a phone. So say what you want in the first two sentences. For example: "I am writing to ask if we can move the meeting to Monday." After that, you can add the details.

Fourth, keep it short. Use short sentences and simple words. If you have a lot to say, use a list or small paragraphs, because a long block of text is hard to read.

Fifth, be clear about what you need. If you need an answer by a certain day, say so. "Could you please reply by Wednesday?" is better than "Please answer soon". If someone must do something, write who, what, and when.

Sixth, check your email before you send it. Read it once more and look for mistakes. A wrong name or a spelling mistake can look careless. Also check the attachments.

Finally, think about your tone. It is easy to sound angry in writing, because the reader cannot hear your voice. If you feel upset, wait an hour before you answer. Words such as "please" and "thank you" cost nothing, and they make a big difference.

Good emails are not difficult to write. With a little practice, you will save time for yourself and for the people who read your messages.
