Most of us use umbrellas without thinking about them. We open one when it rains, and we close it when we get home. But the umbrella has a long and interesting story.

The first umbrellas were not for rain. They were made to give shade from the sun. People in ancient Egypt, China, and Greece used them more than three thousand years ago. In many places, an umbrella was a sign of power. Only kings, priests, and rich people could use one. A servant often held it over their heads.

Rain umbrellas came later. In China, people began to cover umbrellas with paper and put wax or oil on them. This made the paper waterproof. Chinese craftsmen could make light, strong umbrellas that could be folded.

In Europe, umbrellas were slow to become popular. In the seventeenth century, they were used mostly by women. Men thought that carrying an umbrella was not manly. The situation changed in the eighteenth century. A traveller named Jonas Hanway walked around London with an umbrella in all kinds of weather. People laughed at him at first. But after some years, even men started to use them.

Early umbrellas were heavy. They had frames made of wood or whalebone, and they were difficult to carry. In 1852, a man in England invented a steel frame. It made umbrellas much lighter and cheaper, and soon almost everyone could own one.

Today, there are many types. Some are small enough to fit in a bag. Others open with just a button. Some people still prefer the old style, with a wooden handle. Whatever the kind, the umbrella remains one of our simplest and most useful inventions.
