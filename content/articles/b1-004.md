The library was built two years ago by an old teacher named Mr Brown. He lived alone, and he had hundreds of books in his house. One day he said to himself, "These books should not sit on a shelf. People should read them." So he made a box from old wood and put it outside.

At first, only a few people used it. But soon the news spread. Children came after school to look for stories about animals and space. Parents stopped to find a book to read in the evening. Students left their old schoolbooks for younger students.

The library is never empty. Some people bring books they have finished, and others bring books they bought by mistake. Once, someone left a very old cookbook with notes on every page. A woman from the next street took it home and now cooks one recipe every Sunday.

Mr Brown still visits the box every morning. He cleans the glass, puts the books in order, and talks to anyone who is passing by. "I do not really take care of the books," he says. "I take care of the street."

Last winter it snowed for three days, and the library was covered in white. A group of children made a path to the door and put a small umbrella on the roof. Then they put a note inside: "Thank you for the stories."
