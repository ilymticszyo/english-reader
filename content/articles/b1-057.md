Chocolate is one of the most popular foods in the world. We eat it as a snack, as a present, or as a drink on a cold day. But chocolate has a long and interesting history.

It began in Central and South America more than three thousand years ago. People there found the seeds of a tree called the cacao tree. The seeds are bitter, but the people learned to dry them, crush them, and mix them with water. The result was a dark, strong drink. It was not sweet at all.

The old peoples of that region thought that cacao was very special. Their rulers drank it at special times, and in some places, the seeds were even used as money.

About five hundred years ago, European travellers brought cacao home. At first, people in Europe did not like the bitter taste. But then somebody added sugar, and the drink became very popular. For a long time, only rich people could buy it, because the sugar and the cacao were expensive.

Everything changed about two hundred years ago. Machines made it possible to separate the fat from the cacao seeds, and soon, makers found a way to produce hard chocolate that people could eat. Because of the machines, chocolate became cheap, and almost everybody could enjoy it.

Today, most of the world's cacao grows in West Africa, and millions of farmers depend on it. Sadly, many of them are very poor. They receive only a small part of the money that we pay for a chocolate bar. Some companies now work with farmers to give them a fairer price and to stop children from doing dangerous work on farms. When you buy chocolate, you can look for signs on the package that show it was made in a fair way.

Chocolate is also a symbol of love and friendship. People give it on birthdays and holidays all around the world.

So next time you eat a piece, think about its long journey from a bitter seed to your hand.
