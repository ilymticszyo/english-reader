Last summer, I joined a group of volunteers who clean the beach near my town. I had seen the sign many times, but I had never gone. One day, my friend Nora said, "Come with me. It will only take a morning." So I went.

We met at nine o'clock. About forty people were there: students, families with children, and some older men and women. A woman named Ruth gave us gloves, a big plastic bag, and a short talk about safety. "Do not touch anything that looks dangerous," she said. "Call me, and I will help."

At first, the beach looked quite clean from far away. But when I looked at the sand more carefully, I saw small pieces of plastic everywhere. There were bottles, bags, and caps, and thousands of tiny bits that were almost the same colour as the sand. In just one hour, my bag was full. I found a broken toy, an old shoe, and a lot of cigarette ends. My friend Nora found a fishing net that was half buried in the sand.

The children made a game of it. They ran along the beach and shouted every time they found something new. A small boy found a glass bottle with a letter inside.

At noon, we put all the bags in a pile. There were more than eighty. Ruth weighed them on a large scale, and the total was nearly three hundred kilograms. We were tired and our hands were dirty, but everybody was smiling. Somebody brought cold drinks, and we sat on the sand and talked.

The next day, I began to look at my own habits. I decided to take my own bag to the shop and to stop buying water in plastic bottles. These are small changes, but they make a difference.

I will go again next month. It is hard work, but it feels good to do something real.
