In many countries, the year has four parts: spring, summer, autumn, and winter. In summer it is hot and the days are long. In winter it is cold and the days are short. Many people think this is because the Earth is closer to the sun in summer, but this is not true. The real reason is a little more interesting.

The Earth goes around the sun once a year. At the same time, it turns around once every day, like a ball. The Earth does not stand straight, though. It leans a little to one side, like a tower that is not quite straight. This lean is the reason for the seasons.

Because the Earth leans, different parts of it face the sun at different times of the year. In June, the top half of the Earth, called the northern half, leans towards the sun. The sun is high in the sky, the light is strong, and the days are long. It is summer there. At the same time, the bottom half leans away, so it receives less light, and it is winter in places such as Australia and Argentina.

Six months later, in December, everything is the other way round. Now the southern half leans towards the sun, and people there have summer, perhaps with a Christmas on the beach. The northern half leans away, and the days are short and cold.

In the middle of the year, in spring and autumn, no half leans very much towards the sun. The days and nights are almost the same length, and the weather is mild.

What about places near the middle of the Earth, such as Kenya or Singapore? There, the sun is always high in the sky, so it is warm all year. Many of these countries have a wet season and a dry season instead of four seasons.

So next time you feel cold in winter, remember that the Earth is not farther away from the sun. It is only leaning in a different direction.
