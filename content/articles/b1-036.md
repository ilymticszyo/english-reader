The elephant is the biggest animal that lives on land. An adult can weigh as much as six cars, and it can eat more than a hundred kilograms of plants every day. But elephants are not only big. They are also clever, kind, and very good at living together.

Elephants live in families. A family usually has between five and fifteen members, and the leader is the oldest female. People call her the matriarch. She knows where to find water in a dry year, and she remembers places that she visited many years ago. When danger comes, the whole family follows her.

Baby elephants stay close to their mothers. A mother carries her baby for almost two years before it is born, which is longer than any other animal. After birth, the baby cannot use its long nose well. Other females in the family help to look after the young ones, and they keep them safe from lions.

The long nose is very useful. An elephant can use it to drink, to smell, to pick up small food, and to wash itself. It can also make loud sounds. Elephants talk to each other with deep sounds that humans cannot hear, and the sounds can travel for many kilometres.

Scientists have also found that elephants show strong feelings. They stay near a family member who is ill, and they seem sad when one of them dies.

Sadly, elephants have many problems today. People cut down forests where they live, and some people hunt them for their long teeth. In the last hundred years, the number of elephants in Africa has fallen very much. Many countries now have strong laws to protect them, and some groups help farmers and elephants to live in the same area without fighting.

If we want to keep these wonderful animals in the world, we need to take care of their homes and stop the hunting.
