It had been raining since the morning when Mia arrived in Osaka. She stood outside the station with her small bag and looked at the grey sky. She had planned to visit a castle that day, but now she was not sure.

"Maybe I should find a café," she thought. She walked down a busy street and found a small shop with a warm yellow light. Inside, the air smelled of coffee and sweet bread. Mia sat by the window and ordered a cup of tea.

The woman behind the counter smiled at her. "Are you on holiday?" she asked in slow English. Mia said yes, and told her about the castle. The woman laughed. "Today the castle will be very wet! But the museum next door is lovely, and it is free for visitors on rainy days."

Mia had never heard of a rule like that. She finished her tea, thanked the woman, and bought a piece of bread for later. Then she opened her umbrella and walked to the museum.

The museum was quiet and warm. Mia saw old maps, beautiful plates, and a small model of the city from three hundred years ago. An old man stood next to her and pointed at the model. "That is my street," he said with a proud smile. He spoke for ten minutes about how the city had changed.

When Mia left, the rain had stopped. The streets were shining and the air felt fresh. She walked to the castle after all, and it was almost empty. She took many photos of the water, the trees, and the old walls.

That evening, Mia wrote in her notebook: "I came to see a castle, but the best part of my day was the rain. It showed me places I did not plan to visit and people I did not plan to meet."
