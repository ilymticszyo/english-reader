Think about the last time you laughed so much that your stomach hurt. It feels wonderful. But why do human beings laugh? It is a surprisingly difficult question, and scientists are still looking for the answers.

The first thing to know is that laughing is very old. Many scientists believe that it began long before language. Young apes make a kind of laughing sound when they play, and so do some other animals. Even rats make high sounds when someone touches them gently. This suggests that laughter began as a signal that says, "This is only play. I am not going to hurt you."

Laughter is also a way to connect with others. Studies show that we laugh about thirty times more often when we are with other people than when we are alone. And we do not laugh only because of jokes. Most of the time, we laugh in normal conversations, after small and simple sentences, such as "I will see you later". It helps us to show that we are friendly and that we want to be close.

Laughing is also contagious. When you hear someone laugh, you often begin to smile or laugh yourself, even if you do not know why. This is why shows on television sometimes add the sound of people laughing. It makes us feel that something is funny.

There are health benefits, too. A good laugh makes you breathe deeply and moves your muscles. Afterwards, your body feels more relaxed, and the feeling can last for a short time. Some doctors say that laughter lowers stress and can even help people with pain, because the brain makes chemicals that make us feel good.

What makes something funny? Many jokes are about something surprising or something that is not normal. We expect one thing, and then something different happens. The brain enjoys this small shock, and we laugh.

Babies start to laugh when they are about four months old, long before they can speak. It seems that we are all born ready to laugh, and that is a very nice thing to know.
