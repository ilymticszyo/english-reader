My aunt Clara is a nurse in a big hospital, and she has been doing this job for fifteen years. Last week, I asked her to tell me about a normal working day. Her answer made me respect her even more.

Clara's day begins at six in the morning. She gets up in the dark, has a quick breakfast, and takes the first bus. At seven o'clock, she meets the night nurses, who tell her about the patients. "This is the most important part of the morning," she says. "I must know who had a bad night and who needs extra help."

Then she goes round the ward and says good morning to each patient. She checks their temperature, their heart, and their blood pressure, and she writes everything on a form. She gives out medicine at the right time, and she helps people who cannot get out of bed. Some patients are afraid, so she talks to them in a gentle voice. "A kind word can sometimes help as much as a pill," she told me.

In the middle of the morning, the doctors come to see the patients. Clara goes with them and explains what she has seen. She is the person who knows each patient best, because she spends the most time with them.

The afternoon is quieter. She talks to the families of the patients and answers their questions. She also fills in papers on the computer, which she does not enjoy. At half past three, her shift ends, but she often stays longer.

It is a very tiring job. Her feet hurt, and she works at weekends and at night. Some days are sad, because not every patient gets better. But she says that she would not do anything else.

When I asked her what she is proudest of, she thought for a moment. "When someone says thank you and means it," she said.
