Last summer, I got my first real job. I worked in a small bookshop near my university, five days a week. I wanted to earn some money, but I learned much more.

On the first day, I was afraid. There were so many things to learn. I had to know where every book was, use the computer to look for a title, and remember what each customer asked for. My manager, Anna, was patient. She said, "Everyone makes mistakes. Just try again." On that day, I put a book about cooking on the wrong shelf, and I sold a customer the wrong dictionary.

After a week, it was easier. I knew most of the customers' faces and even some of their names. There was an old man who came every morning at eight and always wanted the same newspaper. There was a young mother who came with her baby, and a group of students who sat on the floor for hours with their favourite comics. I enjoyed talking to them, and many of them became friends.

The work was not always easy. On busy days, the line of people reached the door, and my feet hurt at the end of the day. Sometimes a customer was angry because we did not have the book he wanted. At first, this made me upset. Anna taught me to smile, to say sorry, and to offer to order the book for him. She said, "People are often tired, not angry with you."

I also learned about teamwork. When one person was ill, everyone worked a little harder. When it was quiet, we cleaned the shelves together and told jokes.

At the end of August, I said goodbye to everyone. Anna gave me a card, and the old man gave me a small gift. It was a book, of course. I almost cried.

I did not become rich, but I became more confident. Now I know that I can learn new things quickly, and that a good team can make any job more fun.
