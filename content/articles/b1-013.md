Have you ever looked at the sky at night and noticed that the moon is not always the same? One night it is a big, bright circle. A week later it is half a circle, and later it is only a thin line. A few days after that, you cannot see it at all. Why does this happen?

The first thing to know is that the moon does not make its own light. It only shines because the sun lights it, just like a mirror. The sun always lights half of the moon, the half that faces it. But from Earth, we do not always see that half.

The moon goes around the Earth, and it takes about one month to make one trip. While it moves, we look at it from different places. When the moon is between the Earth and the sun, the bright half faces away from us, and we see only the dark half. This is called the new moon. A few days later, a small part of the bright half comes into view, and we see a thin line of light.

Each night, we see a little more. After about a week, we can see half of the bright side. After two weeks, the moon is on the other side of the Earth, and we see the whole bright half. This is the full moon. Then the same thing happens in the opposite direction. The light gets smaller every night, and after another two weeks, the moon is new again.

Many people think that the shadow of the Earth makes the shape of the moon. This is a common mistake. The Earth's shadow only falls on the moon on rare nights, and then we call it an eclipse.

For thousands of years, people have used the moon to count time. The word "month" comes from the word "moon". Many festivals, in many countries, still follow the moon.

So the next time you look up, remember that the moon has not changed. Only your point of view has.
