Almost everyone loves rainbows. They appear after rain, when the sun comes out again. They fill the sky with colour, and they seem a little magical. But it is not an object. You cannot touch it or walk to it. It is made by light and water.

Sunlight looks white, but it is really a mix of many colours. When the light goes through a drop of water, it bends. Each colour bends by a different amount. The light then bounces off the back of the drop and comes out again. This time, the colours are separated and go in different directions.

There are millions of drops in the air after rain. Each one sends colours to your eyes. The drops that send you red light are higher in the sky. The drops that send you purple light are lower. This is why there are bands of colour in a fixed order. Red is always on the outside, and purple is on the inside.

To see one, you need the sun behind you and rain in front of you. If the sun is too high in the sky, you will not see anything. So you usually see them in the morning or late in the afternoon.

Every person sees a slightly different picture. The drops that send light to your eyes are not the same as the drops that send light to another person. In that way, each one belongs to just one person.

Sometimes you can see two at once. The second one is fainter and has the colours in the opposite order. Next time it rains while the sun is shining, turn around and look at the sky.
