Every culture has a way of welcoming the new year. Some traditions are noisy and bright. Others are quiet and calm. But they all have the same hope: that the coming year will be a happy one.

In Japan, people clean their homes before the end of December. They believe that this removes the bad things of the old year. On the last night, temples ring a large bell one hundred and eight times. Many families eat long noodles, which stand for a long life. On the first morning, children often receive small gifts of money.

In Spain, people eat twelve grapes at midnight. They eat one grape with each stroke of the clock. If you finish all twelve, you will have good luck in the new year. It is not easy to do, and it makes everyone laugh.

In Scotland, the new year festival is called Hogmanay. People sing an old song called "Auld Lang Syne" with their arms crossed and their hands joined. The song is about remembering old friends. It is now sung in many other countries as well.

In Brazil, many people go to the beach and wear white clothes. White stands for peace. Some walk into the sea and jump over seven waves, making a wish with each one.

In many parts of the world, fireworks light the sky at midnight. People believe that loud noises scare away bad spirits.

In China, the new year follows the moon, so the date changes each year. Families meet for a big dinner, and they give red envelopes to children.

These customs are very different, but they show how much people love to begin again.
