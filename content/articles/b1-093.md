Last year, my company told us that we could work from home. I was excited. No more crowded trains in the morning! I imagined that I would wake up late, drink coffee slowly, and finish my work easily. The first week taught me that it was not so simple.

On the first day, I sat on my sofa with my laptop. After an hour, my back hurt. I also found it hard to concentrate. The television was only a few steps away, and my kitchen had too many snacks. By the end of the day, I had done very little.

So I made some rules. First, I cleared a small table near the window and made it my desk. I bought a good chair, and I put a plant next to my computer. When I sit there, my mind knows that it is time for work.

Second, I started to follow a regular schedule. I wake up at seven, take a shower, and get dressed, even though nobody can see me. I begin work at nine and take a lunch break at noon. At six, I turn off my laptop and close the door of my work corner.

Third, I try to talk to my colleagues. At first, I felt lonely. Now we have a short video call every morning, and we sometimes talk about our weekends. I also go for a walk after lunch.

Now I enjoy working from home. I save time and money, and I have more time to cook. But I still go to the office once a week. I miss the lunches with my team, and a little change is good for everyone.
