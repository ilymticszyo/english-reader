When I was fifteen, my uncle took me to a sushi restaurant for the first time. I was nervous. I knew that sushi was a famous food, but I was not sure I wanted to eat raw fish.

The restaurant was small and quiet. There was a long wooden counter, and the chef stood behind it. He smiled at us and said hello in a low voice. My uncle ordered for both of us, and I watched the chef work. His hands moved very fast. He pressed the rice into small shapes and put a thin piece of fish on top of each one.

The first plate came, and my uncle told me how to eat it. "You can use your fingers," he said. "Do not put too much soy sauce on it. Just touch the fish to the sauce, not the rice." I did as he said.

I picked up a piece of tuna and put it in my mouth. It was soft, and it tasted fresh and a little sweet. I was surprised. It was nothing like I imagined. I wanted to try more.

Next, I ate egg, shrimp, and a roll with cucumber. The roll was my favourite. I did not like the sea urchin, which was very strong and creamy, but I was happy that I tried it.

At the end, the chef gave us hot tea. My uncle said, "In this kind of restaurant, the chef takes years to learn his skill. It is good to show thanks." So I said "thank you" to the chef, and he nodded and smiled.

Now I eat sushi often. Every time, I remember that quiet restaurant and my uncle's advice.
