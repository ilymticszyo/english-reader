On a spring morning, before the sun comes up, you can often hear a lot of birds singing. It sounds beautiful, but the birds are not singing for our pleasure. Scientists believe that they sing for two main reasons.

The first reason is to find a partner. In most kinds of birds, it is the male that sings. He wants to show a female that he is strong and healthy. A bird that can sing a long, difficult song is probably in good condition, so females often choose the best singers.

The second reason is to protect a territory. A territory is a piece of land with food and a safe place for a nest. A bird sings from a high place, to tell other birds, "This place is mine. Keep away." In this way, the birds avoid fights.

Birds also use short sounds, which we call calls. These are different from songs. A call can mean "Danger!" or "Here is some food!" or "Where are you?" Small birds often have a special call for danger. When one bird sees a cat or a hawk, it makes a loud, sharp sound, and all the others in the area fly away at once.

Young birds must learn to sing. They listen to their fathers and try to copy them. At first, their songs are not very good, and they make many mistakes. But with practice, they improve.

Why do birds sing so much in the early morning? One idea is that the air is quiet at that time, so the sound travels farther.

Sadly, in big cities, birds must sing louder, because of the noise from cars and machines. Scientists have found that some city birds sing higher, so that other birds can hear them.

Next time you hear birds in the morning, stop and listen. They are telling each other many important things.
