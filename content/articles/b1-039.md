Every four years, athletes from all over the world meet to take part in the Olympic Games. Millions of people watch on television. But the first games were very different from what we see today. They began in Greece about 2,800 years ago.

The ancient games took place in a town called Olympia. It was a holy place, and the games were part of a festival for Zeus, the most important of the old gods. The games were so important that wars stopped while they happened. Visitors could travel safely to the town, even if their countries were fighting.

At first, there was only one event: a running race of about two hundred metres. Later, other events were added, such as longer races, jumping, and throwing a heavy ball. There were also horse races.

Only men could take part. Women were not allowed to watch, and some were not even allowed to enter Olympia. The athletes did not wear any clothes.

The winners did not receive gold medals. They got a simple crown made from the leaves of an olive tree. But for the Greeks, it was a great honour. A winner became famous in his home town.

The ancient games continued for more than a thousand years. In the end, a new ruler decided to close them, because they were part of an old religion. Then, for a very long time, there were no games.

The modern games began in 1896, when a French man, Pierre de Coubertin, started them again in Athens. At first, only a few countries took part. Now more than two hundred countries send athletes, and both men and women compete in many sports.

The Olympic fire, which people carry from Greece to the city of the games, reminds us of the first festival.
