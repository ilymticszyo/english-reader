Almost everything we do online depends on a password. It protects our email, our bank account, our photographs, and our social media. Yet many people treat passwords carelessly, and criminals take advantage of this. A few simple habits can make a big difference to your safety.
 
First, avoid obvious choices. Studies of leaked data show that the most common passwords are still very simple, such as strings of numbers in order or the word "password" itself. Others use the name of a pet or a child, which are easy for strangers to guess or to find on social media. A good password is long and unpredictable. Experts now recommend using several random words together, for example, four unrelated words, because length is more important than complicated symbols.
 
Second, never use the same password for different accounts. When a website is attacked, criminals often publish the stolen email addresses and passwords. They then try them on other sites, knowing that many people repeat them. If you use the same password everywhere, one leak could give a thief access to your whole digital life.
 
But remembering dozens of different passwords is impossible. This is where a password manager helps. It is a program that stores all your passwords in a protected place, and it fills them in automatically when you need them. You need to remember only one strong password to open it. Many security experts regard these programs as the safest and most practical solution for ordinary people.
 
Third, turn on two-step verification wherever possible. After you enter your password, the service sends a code to your phone, or asks you to confirm using an app. Even if a criminal steals your password, they cannot get in without the second step. This is one of the most effective protections available.
 
Fourth, watch for tricks. Many attacks do not break any computer system. They simply persuade people to give away their details. An email may claim to come from your bank and urge you to click a link and enter your password. The message may look perfectly real. Be suspicious of messages that create a sense of urgency, and never click a link in an unexpected message. Instead, go directly to the website by typing its address.
 
Finally, keep your devices up to date. Updates fix security problems, and criminals use known weaknesses in old software.
 
None of this takes long, and most of it needs to be done only once. A little care now can prevent the stress and cost of losing your accounts, your money, or your private memories.
