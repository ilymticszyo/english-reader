Doctors often say, "Move more." But why is exercise so important? It is not only about looking good or getting strong. Exercise helps almost every part of the body, and it also helps the mind.

First, exercise is good for the heart. When you walk fast or run, your heart beats faster, and it gets stronger. A strong heart can pump blood with less effort. This lowers the risk of many illnesses, including heart disease.

Exercise also helps the bones and muscles. When we use our muscles, they grow stronger. When we walk or jump, our bones become harder. This is very important as we grow older, because weak bones can break easily. People who stay active can often keep their strength well into old age.

Another benefit is better sleep. People who move during the day usually fall asleep more quickly and sleep more deeply. They also wake up feeling fresher.

Exercise is very good for the mind, too. When you exercise, your body makes chemicals that make you feel happier. That is why a walk can help when you feel stressed or sad. Studies show that active people have fewer problems with worry.

How much do we need? Experts say that adults should do about thirty minutes of moderate exercise on most days of the week. Moderate means that you breathe a little harder, but you can still talk. Fast walking, cycling, and swimming are all good choices.

You do not have to join a gym. Take the stairs. Get off the bus one stop early. Dance in your living room. Small actions add up. The best exercise is the one that you enjoy, because that is the one you will keep doing.
