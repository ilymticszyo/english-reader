Every Saturday morning, there is a market in the square near my house. Farmers from the villages around the city bring their food, and people come from all parts of the town to buy it. I go almost every week, and it is one of the best parts of my weekend.

The market opens at eight o'clock, but I like to arrive a little earlier. At that time, the square is still quiet, and the sellers are putting out their things. The air smells of fresh bread, flowers, and coffee. I always start by buying a warm roll from a small stall, and I eat it while I walk around.

There are many kinds of stalls. One man sells only potatoes, in six different colours. A woman has big boxes of red and yellow tomatoes, and she lets you taste one before you buy. Another stall has eggs, milk, and cheese from her own cows. There is also a fish stall, and a very friendly old couple who sell honey and jam. Everything is fresh, and most of it was in the ground or on the tree the day before.

I like to talk to the sellers. They know their food very well. They tell me how to cook it, which fruit is best this week, and what is not ready yet. If I do not know a vegetable, they explain it to me and give me an easy idea.

Food at the market is sometimes a little more expensive than in the supermarket. But I think it is worth it. It tastes better, and the money goes straight to the person who grew it.

By eleven o'clock, my bags are full and heavy, and I walk home slowly. In the afternoon, I cook something with the things I bought. It is a simple pleasure, but it makes me feel happy and close to the place where I live.
