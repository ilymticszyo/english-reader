Last Saturday, I took my two nephews to the science museum. Jack is nine years old, and his sister Emma is seven. They were both excited, but I was a little worried. I thought that they would get bored after half an hour.

I was wrong. As soon as we went in, the children ran to the big hall with the dinosaur bones. Emma stood with her mouth open and looked up at a huge skeleton. "How can an animal be so big?" she asked. A friendly worker told us that this dinosaur was as long as three buses, and that it ate only plants. Jack wanted to know how scientists could find out all of this from old bones. The man gave a long, interesting answer, and the children listened carefully.

After that, we went to the room about the human body. There was a big model of a heart that you could walk through, and a game where you had to test your own reaction speed. Jack beat me three times, which made him very proud. Emma liked the part where you could listen to your own heartbeat.

In the afternoon, we visited the space room. We saw a real rocket and a small piece of the moon. Jack stood next to a model of an astronaut and said that he wants to go to space one day. We also tried a machine that shows how much you would weigh on other planets. On Mars, Emma was almost as light as a feather, and she loved it.

At closing time, the children did not want to leave. We bought two small books in the shop, and on the way home, Emma fell asleep in the car.

I learned that museums today are not quiet and serious. They are places where children can touch, play, and try things for themselves. A good museum can make a child curious, and curiosity is the start of learning. I am already planning our next visit.
