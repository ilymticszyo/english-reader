You can live for weeks without food, but you can live for only a few days without water. About sixty percent of an adult's body is water. It is in your blood, your muscles, and even your bones. Without it, your body cannot work.

Water has many jobs. First, it helps to keep your body at the right temperature. When it is hot, you sweat, and the water on your skin takes heat away. This is why you must drink more on a hot day or when you play sport. Second, water helps you to digest food, and it carries the good parts to the places that need them.

Water is also very important for your brain. If you do not drink enough, you may feel tired, have a headache, and find it difficult to think. Even a small loss of water, just one or two percent, can change how you feel.

How much water do you need? There is no number that is right for everybody. A common idea is eight glasses a day, which is about two litres. But the amount depends on your size, the weather, and how active you are. You also get water from food. Fruit and vegetables, such as oranges, cucumbers, and tomatoes, have a lot of it. Tea, coffee, and soup also count.

A simple way to know if you are drinking enough is to look at the colour of your urine. It should be light yellow. If it is dark, you need more water.

Here are some easy tips. Keep a bottle on your desk. Drink a glass when you wake up and another before each meal. If you do not like the taste, add a few pieces of lemon or fruit.

Do not wait until you feel thirsty. When you feel thirsty, your body is already asking for help. A little water, often during the day, is the best way to stay healthy.
