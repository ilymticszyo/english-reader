Last Saturday I invited four friends to my flat for dinner. I had never cooked for so many people before, and I was very nervous. For a week I had looked at recipes online, and in the end I chose a simple menu: tomato soup, chicken with rice, and a fruit cake.

On Saturday morning I went to the market. I bought fresh tomatoes, onions, garlic, and a big chicken. The man at the vegetable stall gave me some free herbs and said, "Good luck with your dinner!" That made me feel a little better.

By four o'clock the kitchen was a mess. There were pots on the table, onions on the floor, and flour on my shirt. I started the soup first, because the recipe said it needed an hour. Then I put the chicken in the oven and began to make the cake.

Then something went wrong. I forgot to turn on the oven for the cake, and I remembered only when my friends arrived. I felt terrible. But my friend Ken laughed and said, "Let's eat the soup and the chicken first. The cake can wait."

The soup was a bit too salty, but everyone said it was good. The chicken was perfect, and my friends asked for more rice. While we ate, we talked about work, music, and our plans for the summer. I forgot about the mess in the kitchen.

At nine o'clock we finally ate the cake. It was still warm, and it was the best part of the evening. Nobody went home until midnight.

Now I know that cooking for friends is not about making perfect food. It is about sharing time together. Next month, I want to cook again, and this time I will write a list so I do not forget the oven.
