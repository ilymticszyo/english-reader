A large plane can weigh as much as fifty elephants. It is hard to believe that something so heavy can fly. But planes fly every day, and it is not magic. It is science.

There are four main forces that act on a plane. The first is weight, which pulls the plane down towards the ground. The second is lift, which pushes it up. The third is thrust, which pushes it forward. The fourth is drag, which is the force of the air that slows the plane down. To fly, the plane needs enough lift to be stronger than its weight, and enough thrust to be stronger than drag.

Let us start with lift, which comes from the wings. The wing has a special shape. It is round on the top and flatter on the bottom. When the plane moves fast, the air goes over and under the wing. The wing also pushes the air down a little. Because of this, the air pushes the wing up. It is like putting your hand out of a car window. If you turn your hand a little, the wind lifts it.

For the plane to make enough lift, it must move very fast, and this is the job of the engines. They make thrust by pushing a lot of air backwards. Because of the engines, a big plane moves along the ground at very fast before it leaves the ground.

Once the plane is in the air, the pilot has several ways to control it. Parts on the wings and at the back move up and down, and they let the pilot turn the plane, go higher, or come down.

Flying is also very safe. Today, it is much safer than going by car.

So the next time you look out of the window at a wing, remember what it is doing. It is quietly pushing the air down, so that you can stay high above the clouds.
