Some machines turn sunlight into electricity. You can see them on roofs, in fields, and even on small things like calculators. But how does light become power?

These machines are made of many small flat parts. Each part is a thin piece of a material called silicon. It is common, and it is found in sand. When it is made very clean and cut into thin slices, it can react to light.

Sunlight is made of tiny pieces of energy. When these pieces hit the material, they push small things called electrons so that they begin to move. If we connect wires, the electrons flow through the wires. A flow of electrons is an electric current. This current can power a light, a fan, or a computer.

The electricity from the sun is of one type. Homes use another type. So the system needs a small machine to change one type into the other. After that, the power can be used in the home. Extra power can go to the public network, or it can be stored in a large battery for use at night.

The system works best on a clear, sunny day, but it also makes some power when the sky is cloudy. It does not need to be hot. In fact, very high heat makes it a little less effective.

Sun power has big advantages. It makes no smoke and no noise, and the sun gives us free energy every day. The machines last twenty-five years or more. Their price has fallen a lot over the last ten years, so more and more families can use them.
