Many parents read stories to their children before bed. It is a pleasant habit, and it also does a lot of good. Teachers and doctors agree that reading aloud is one of the best things you can do for a child.

First, it helps children learn language. When a child hears a story, he or she hears many new words. Books often use words that we do not use in daily talk. A child who listens to stories every day will know more words when starting school.

Reading aloud also builds a love of books. If a child connects stories with warm, happy moments, he or she will want to read later. A parent's voice makes a story come alive. Children enjoy the different voices for different characters, and they often ask for the same book again and again. This is normal. Repeating a story helps them learn.

Another benefit is a closer relationship. For ten or fifteen minutes, the parent and child sit together, and they have each other's full attention. There are no phones and no television. Many adults say that these quiet times are among their best memories of childhood.

How can you make reading aloud more fun? Choose books that match the child's interests. Stop sometimes and ask questions, such as "What do you think will happen next?" Let the child turn the pages. And do not worry if you are not a perfect reader. What matters is that you are there.

You can keep reading aloud even when children can read alone. Many older children still enjoy listening, and it is a good way to share a long book.
