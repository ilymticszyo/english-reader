When I moved into my flat three years ago, I did not know my neighbours. In the city, many people never speak to the people next door. But one of my neighbours changed this, and he did it with music.

His name is Mr. Ito, and he is about seventy years old. He lives alone on the floor above me. At first, I only knew him from the sounds. Every evening at seven o'clock, I could hear a violin. Sometimes the music was slow and sad, and sometimes it was fast and happy. Sometimes there were mistakes, and he played the same part again and again.

At first, I was a little annoyed, because I wanted quiet after work. But after a few weeks, something changed. I started to wait for the music. When it did not come, the evening felt empty.

One day, I met Mr. Ito in the lift. I said that I liked his playing. He looked very surprised. "You can hear me?" he said. "I am sorry. I will play more quietly." I told him not to stop. His face became bright, and he said that nobody had told him this in years. He had been a music teacher, but after he stopped working, he thought nobody wanted to listen.

After that day, we became friends. Sometimes I took him a cup of tea, and he told me stories about his life and his students. Once he invited me to his flat, and I sat in a big chair while he played a whole song just for me.

Then I had an idea. I asked the other neighbours if they wanted to listen. On a warm evening in June, we all sat in the garden behind our building, and Mr. Ito played for almost an hour. Children, old people, and families came. Nobody spoke, and when he finished, everyone clapped.

Now we have a small concert every month. People bring food and chairs. Because of one old violin, our building has become a small community.
