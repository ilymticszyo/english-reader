In autumn, many trees put on a beautiful show. Their green leaves turn yellow, orange, or red. Then they fall to the ground. Why does this happen?

To understand it, we first need to know why leaves are green. Leaves contain a special substance called chlorophyll. It gives them their green colour. More importantly, it helps the tree make food from sunlight, air, and water. The tree uses this food to grow.

In spring and summer, there is plenty of light, and the leaves work hard. The tree makes new chlorophyll all the time. The leaves stay bright green.

In autumn, the days become shorter and cooler. There is less sunlight, so the tree slowly stops making chlorophyll. The green colour fades away. Now we can see other colours that were in the leaf all along. Yellow and orange were hidden under the green.

The red colour is different. Some trees make a new red substance in autumn. This happens when the days are sunny and the nights are cool. That is why the red colours are often brightest after a dry, sunny autumn.

After the colours change, the tree closes the place where the leaf joins the branch. The leaf cannot get water any more, and it falls off. This helps the tree save water and energy in the cold winter. Without leaves, the tree can rest.

In some countries, many people travel just to see autumn leaves. They walk in forests and parks and take photographs. If you live near trees, take a walk this autumn. Look carefully, and you will see how much beauty there is in a single leaf.
