The writing is small and careful. She did not use exact measures. Instead, she wrote things like "a handful of flour" or "salt, a little." At first, this made cooking difficult for me. I did not know how big her hand was! But I asked my mother, and she showed me how my grandmother cooked. After a while, I learned to understand the notes.

Some of the pages have little comments. Next to a recipe for fish soup, she wrote, "Your grandfather's favourite." Next to a cake, she wrote, "Too sweet. Use less sugar next time." I like to imagine her standing in the kitchen with a pencil, trying again and again until the cake was perfect.

My favourite recipe is her potato stew. It is warm and simple, and it smells like my childhood. When I make it, the whole house smells the same as her house did. I feel as if she is standing next to me.

I want to give this gift to my own children one day. I have started to copy the recipes into a new notebook, and I add my own notes. Food is more than something to eat. It is a way to keep a family together, even when we are far apart.
