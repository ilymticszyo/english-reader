Today, we use money every day. We have coins and notes in our pockets, and cards in our bags. We can even pay with our phones. But money has not always existed. How did people buy and sell things before it was invented?

In the earliest times, people did not need money. Families made their own food and clothes. Later, when people began to live in larger groups, they started to trade. A farmer who had too much rice could give some of it to a friend who had extra fish. This way of trading is called barter.

Barter works, but it is not easy. Imagine you have a chicken and you want a pair of shoes. The shoemaker may not want a chicken. Perhaps he wants salt, or a knife. You must find someone who has what you need and also wants what you have. This takes a lot of time.

To make trade easier, people began to use things that everybody wanted and accepted. In different parts of the world, these were shells, salt, cloth, or animals such as cows. Later, people found that metals such as gold and silver were very good for this purpose. They are rare, they are beautiful, and they do not break easily.

The first real coins appeared about 2,600 years ago in a country called Lydia, in what is now Turkey. They were small pieces of metal with a picture on them. The picture showed that the ruler promised the coin was worth a certain amount. Soon, other countries made their own.

Paper money came later. China used it more than a thousand years ago, because it was much lighter than a bag of coins. In Europe, paper money became common much later.

Today, more and more of our money is only numbers on a screen. We pay without touching anything. But the basic idea has not changed. Money works because people trust it. If we all believe that a piece of paper or a number on a phone is worth something, then it is.
