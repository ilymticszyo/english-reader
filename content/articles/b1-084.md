Everyone yawns. Babies do it before they are born. Dogs and cats do it, and even fish do it. But scientists still do not fully understand why.

One old idea says that we yawn when we need more air. A yawn is a deep breath, so it seems to make sense. But experiments do not support this idea. In one test, people breathed extra air, and they still yawned just as often.

Another idea is that it helps to cool the brain. The brain works best at a certain temperature, and it makes heat as it works. When we open our mouths wide, we take in a lot of cool air. This may help the brain stay cool. Some studies show that people do it less in very hot weather and more when the air is cool. This fits the idea well.

We also do it when we are tired or bored. Perhaps it helps the body wake up a little. It may also be a signal that tells our brain to pay attention.

The strangest fact is that it is catching. When you see someone else do it, you often want to do it too. You may even want to when you read or talk about it. Some people are starting right now!

Scientists think that catching it may be linked to feeling close to other people. Children under four years old do not usually catch it. Adults catch it more easily from friends and family than from strangers.

So the next time you feel it coming, do not feel shy. It is natural, and it may even be good for you.
