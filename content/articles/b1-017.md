Last week I went to a restaurant with some friends. We were five people at one table, but for most of the evening, nobody spoke. Everyone was looking at a phone. One person was reading messages, another was watching a video, and a third was taking a photo of the food. I felt sad, and I asked myself, "Why did we come here together?"

I like technology, and I use my phone every day. It helps me find new places, talk to people far away, and learn many things. But I believe that dinner is a special time, and phones should stay in our pockets.

First, a meal is a chance to talk. In our busy lives, we do not often sit down with our family and friends and listen to each other. When a phone is on the table, we do not give the other person our full attention. Even a short message can break a conversation, and it is difficult to start again.

Second, phones can make people feel unimportant. If you are talking and your friend is looking at a screen, you may feel that your words do not matter. Many studies show that people enjoy a meal more when no phones are near them.

Third, we taste our food better when we pay attention. Try it: eat your next dinner slowly, without any screen. You will notice colours, smells, and tastes that you usually miss.

Some people say that they need their phones for work or for their children. This is a fair point, and it is fine to answer an important call. But most messages can wait for an hour.

Here is an idea that some families use. At the beginning of the meal, everyone puts their phone in the middle of the table, face down. The first person to touch a phone has to pay for the dessert, or wash the dishes!

It is a small change, but it can make a big difference. Our time together is short, so let us enjoy it.
