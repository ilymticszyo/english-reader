Imagine a forest where it rains almost every day, where the air is warm and wet, and where the trees are as tall as a very big building. This is a rainforest. These forests grow in warm places near the middle of the Earth, such as in South America, Africa, and Southeast Asia.

A rainforest has several layers. At the top, the tallest trees rise above everything else, and they get all the sunshine. Below them is a thick roof of leaves. Most of the animals live here, such as birds, monkeys, and snakes. Underneath, it is dark and damp.

Rainforests are home to an amazing number of living things. They cover only a small part of the Earth, but more than half of all the kinds of plants and animals live in them. New kinds are still being found every year. Many of our medicines come from rainforest plants, and scientists believe that many more are still waiting to be discovered.

Rainforests are also important for the whole planet. Their trees take in a lot of carbon dioxide, the gas that makes the Earth warmer. They also help to make the rain. In this way, they help to control the weather, far beyond the forest.

Sadly, rainforests are in danger. People cut down the trees to get wood or to make space for farms and roads. Every year, an area as large as a small country disappears. When the forest is gone, the animals lose their homes, and the soil, which is not as rich as it looks, soon becomes poor.

What can we do? We can buy products, such as paper and wood, from companies that take care of the forest. We can also support groups that protect these places.

The rainforest is a treasure that we cannot easily replace. If we lose it, we lose far more than trees.
