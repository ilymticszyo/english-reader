In many of the world's large cities, one problem comes up in almost every conversation: the cost of housing. Young people live with their parents into their thirties, families spend more than half of their income on rent, and workers such as nurses and teachers cannot afford to live in the places where they are needed. How did this happen, and what can be done?

The basic cause is simple. In places where many people want to live, there are not enough homes. Cities are attractive because they offer jobs, schools, and entertainment, and so more people arrive every year. But in many cities, the number of new buildings has not kept pace. When demand is higher than supply, prices rise.

Why is it so difficult to build more? One reason is regulation. In many places, strict rules limit the height of buildings, require large amounts of parking, or prevent anything but single-family houses in large areas. The process of getting permission can take years, and local residents often oppose new projects. Many of these people already own their homes, and they worry about noise, traffic, and the loss of the character of the neighbourhood. They also have a financial interest, since scarcity keeps the value of their own property high.

Other factors make matters worse. Land is expensive, building materials have become more costly, and interest rates affect how much people can borrow. In some tourist cities, many flats are rented to visitors rather than residents. In others, investors buy property as a way to store money, and leave it empty.

Cities have tried different solutions. Some have set limits on rent increases. This helps the people who already have a flat, but economists warn that it can discourage owners from building or repairing, and so it may reduce supply in the long term. Others have built social housing, which is owned by the government and rented at low prices. This works well when enough of it is built, as in Vienna, where a large share of residents live in such homes. But it needs large amounts of public money and land.

Many experts argue that the most effective answer is to build more, and to make it easier to do so. This can mean allowing taller buildings near stations, permitting smaller flats, and shortening the approval process. Some countries have recently changed the rules to allow more homes on land that was previously reserved for single houses, and early results suggest that rents have risen more slowly.

There is no single solution, and each city has its own history and needs. But one lesson appears everywhere: a place that makes it hard for people to find homes will, sooner or later, also find it hard to keep its workers, its young people, and its life.
