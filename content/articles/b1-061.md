Today, most of us write messages on our phones. But every day, millions of paper letters still travel around the world. Have you ever wondered how a letter gets from your hand to the house of a friend?

First, you write the address on the envelope and put on a stamp. The stamp shows that you have paid. Then you put the letter in a red box in the street, or you take it to a post office.

Every day, a postal worker opens the box and takes all the letters to a big sorting centre. This is a very large building, often near a city. At night, the centre is full of machines. They take pictures of each envelope, read the address, and send the letter to the correct place. A machine can sort more than thirty thousand letters in one hour. If the writing is difficult to read, a person looks at it and decides.

When the letters are sorted, they are put in boxes and sent to the right city. They travel by lorry, by train, or by plane, depending on the distance. A letter that goes to another country often flies. At the airport, workers load the bags onto the plane, and a few hours later, the letter arrives on the other side of the world.

Then it goes to another sorting centre, where it is sorted again, this time by street. Early in the morning, a postman or postwoman puts the letters in order for the walk or the ride. They carry heavy bags on their backs or in small vans, and they deliver the letters in rain, snow, and heat.

Finally, your friend finds your letter in the box by the door. The whole trip may take only one day in the same country, or about a week to another country.

It is a wonderful system. For a very small price, a person can send a message to almost any house in the world.
