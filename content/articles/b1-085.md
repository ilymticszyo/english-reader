When I was twelve, I was not a good student. I was tired of homework, and I often forgot to bring my books. My marks were low, and I thought that I was just not clever. I wanted to stop trying.

Then I met Mr. Tanaka, my new maths teacher. He was a tall, quiet man with grey hair. He did not shout at students. Instead, he walked around the room and looked at our work. He often said, "Mistakes are good. They show you where to learn."

One day, he returned our test. My mark was the lowest in the class. I put the paper in my bag and did not look at it. After class, Mr. Tanaka asked me to stay. I was sure that he was angry.

But he smiled and said, "I looked at your test carefully. You did the hard questions well. You only made simple mistakes with numbers. That means you understand maths. You just need to slow down." I could not believe it. Nobody had ever said that to me.

He then showed me a way to check my work. I learned to read each question twice and to write every step. It felt slow at first. But my marks went up little by little.

At the end of the year, I got the best mark in a test. Mr. Tanaka put a small star next to my name. I still remember how happy I felt.

Now, I am a teacher too. When my students make mistakes, I think of Mr. Tanaka. I try to say what he said to me, and I hope it helps them as much as it helped me.
