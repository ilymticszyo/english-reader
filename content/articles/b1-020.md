For thousands of years, people looked at birds and dreamed of flying. Many tried, but none succeeded. Then, in 1903, two brothers from the United States did what no one had done before.

Wilbur and Orville Wright were not rich or famous. They owned a small shop where they repaired and built bicycles. But they were very interested in flying, and they read everything they could find. They watched birds for hours, and they saw that birds move the ends of their wings to turn in the air. This gave the brothers an important idea.

They built a long box with a fan, and tested about two hundred different wing shapes. At that time, nobody had made such careful tests. They also built their own light engine, because no company could sell them one that was good enough.

In December 1903, the brothers took their machine to a place called Kitty Hawk, on the coast. The wind there was strong and steady, and the sand was soft. On a cold morning, Orville lay on the lower wing, and the engine started. The machine rolled along a wooden track, rose into the air, and flew for twelve seconds. It travelled about thirty-seven metres. This was the first time in history that a person had flown in a machine with an engine.

They made three more flights that day. The longest one lasted nearly a minute. Only a few people were there to watch, and at first, most newspapers did not believe the story.

In the next years, the brothers continued to improve their machine. By 1908, they were showing it in Europe, and thousands of people came to see it.

Today, millions of people fly every day. It all began on a cold beach, with two brothers who were not afraid to try.
