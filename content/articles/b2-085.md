When a stadium fills with tens of thousands of cheering fans, we expect to see a football match or a concert. Increasingly, though, the spectators have come to watch people playing video games. Competitive gaming, known as esports, has grown from a small hobby into a global industry, with huge audiences and professional players.
 
The roots go back to the early days of arcade machines, when players competed for the highest score. In the 1990s, fans began to organise tournaments for popular computer games, and the spread of fast internet connections made it possible to play against opponents in other countries. Later, websites for live video allowed anyone to watch top players at work. Today, the biggest tournaments are watched online by millions of people and offer prizes worth many millions of dollars.
 
Professional teams now resemble those in traditional sports. They have coaches, managers, and sponsors, and the best players are paid salaries. Many train for ten hours a day or more, practising their reactions and studying their opponents. Careers tend to be short, since quick reflexes decline with age, and many players retire in their late twenties.
 
Supporters of esports point out that it requires real skill. Players must make rapid decisions, cooperate with team-mates, and keep calm under pressure. Some universities now offer scholarships for gifted players, and schools in several countries have started gaming clubs that teach teamwork and strategy. Esports can also be more open than other sports. Players do not need to be physically strong or tall, and people with disabilities can compete on equal terms.
 
Critics, however, raise several concerns. Spending long hours in front of a screen can harm health, causing back pain, eye strain, and lack of sleep. There are worries about the effect on young people, especially if games are designed to keep players hooked. The industry has also been criticised for a lack of diversity, as women are rarely seen among the top professionals, and many report harassment online.
 
There is also debate about whether esports should be called a sport at all. Traditionalists argue that a sport requires physical exertion. Others reply that chess and shooting are accepted as sports, and that the training and mental effort of esports players are comparable to those of any athlete. The question may matter less than it seems. What is clear is that millions of people find these contests exciting.
 
The future looks bright, but it is uncertain. The industry depends on a few large game companies, whose decisions can make or break a competition, and the interest of fans can change quickly. Still, esports shows how technology can create new forms of entertainment, and new communities, almost overnight.
