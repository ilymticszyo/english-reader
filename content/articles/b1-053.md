Have you ever swallowed some water at the beach and made a sour face? Sea water tastes very salty. But the water in rivers and lakes does not. So where does the salt in the sea come from?

The story begins with rain. Rain slowly breaks tiny pieces from rocks. Some of these pieces are salts and other minerals. The rain then washes them into streams, and the streams carry them down to the rivers.

Rivers flow to the sea, and they bring these minerals with them. The amount in each river is very small. If you drink river water, you cannot taste the salt. But rivers have been doing this for millions of years.

There is another important point. When the sun warms the sea, some of the water changes into vapour and goes up into the sky. It forms clouds, and later it comes down as rain. But the salt does not go up. It stays in the sea. So the water is always moving, but the salt stays behind, and the sea becomes a little saltier over time.

You may now ask: if rivers add salt every day, will the sea become more and more salty? Scientists say that it stays almost the same. This is because the salt also leaves the sea in different ways. Some sea animals use it to build their shells, and some of it falls to the bottom of the sea and becomes part of the ground.

The salt in the sea is very important for us. People have taken salt from sea water for thousands of years. We use it every day to cook and to keep food fresh.

So the next time you taste sea water, remember that you are tasting the history of the rocks and rivers of the world.
