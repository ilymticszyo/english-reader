Ten years ago, my mother told me, "Never talk to people you do not know on the internet." Today, she has friends in four countries, and she met all of them online. Times have changed. But is it really possible to make good friends through a screen?

I think the answer is yes, and I have my own story. When I started to learn the guitar, I felt alone. None of my friends played an instrument. So I joined a group online for people who love guitars. At first, I only read the messages. Then I asked a question, and a man from Canada answered it. We began to write every week. We sent each other videos of our playing, and we gave each other advice. After two years, we are good friends, even though we have never met.

There are some good things about online friendships. One good thing is that you can find people with the same interests as you, even if they live very far away. In a small town, it can be hard to find someone who likes the same things.

But there are also dangers. Not everybody is honest. Some people use a false name or picture, and a few want to take your money. So you must be careful. Never give your address, your bank number, or personal photos to someone you do not know well. If a person asks for money, even for a good reason, say no.

I also think online friends cannot replace real ones, because you cannot share a meal through a screen. For me, the best friendships are the ones that use both: we talk online, and, when possible, we also meet in person.

My Canadian friend and I now plan to meet next summer. I am a little nervous, but very happy. In the end, a friend is a friend, wherever he lives.
