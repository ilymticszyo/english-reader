If you have a cat, you know the sound. It is soft and low, like a very small engine. When you touch a cat gently, it often begins to make this sound. For most people, it means that the cat is happy. But is that the whole story?

Cats make this sound by using the muscles in their necks. The muscles move very fast, and the air moves through, and this makes the noise. A cat can do it while breathing in and while breathing out, so the sound goes on without a break.

Of course, cats do it when they feel calm and safe. A kitten starts only a few days after it is born. It does it while it drinks milk from its mother. The mother also does it, and this tells her babies that she is close. So it may be a way of saying, "I am here."

But cats sometimes make the same sound in other situations. Some cats do it when they are afraid, ill, or hurt. A cat at the animal doctor may do it loudly even though it is not enjoying the visit. Scientists think that it may help cats to calm themselves, just as people sometimes sing quietly when they are nervous.

There is another interesting idea. The sound is very low. Some studies say that low sounds of this kind may help bones and muscles to heal. If this is true, it might help a cat to stay healthy.

Next time your cat does this, listen carefully. It may be happy, or it may be trying to tell you something. Look at its eyes and body, and you will understand more.
