Last winter, I decided to do something useful with my free time. A friend told me about a food bank in our town, and I went to see it. A food bank collects food and gives it to people who cannot afford enough. I was not sure that I could help, but they welcomed me warmly.

On Saturday mornings, I work with about ten other volunteers. Some of them are students, and some are retired. There is a man who used to be a bus driver, and a woman who was a nurse. We start at nine o'clock. First, we open the boxes that people have given. There are cans of beans, bags of rice, pasta, and sometimes fresh vegetables from local farms.

We check the dates on each item and put the food on long shelves. Then we make boxes for families. Each box has enough food for a few days. A team leader gives us a list so that every family gets the same amount.

At noon, the doors open. People come in slowly. Some are shy, and some look tired. I learned that many of them have jobs, but their money does not last until the end of the month. Others are elderly, and their pensions are small. One mother told me that her children were now eating better. I will never forget her smile.

At first, I thought I was only giving something to others. But I also receive a lot. I have new friends, and I feel that I am part of my community.

If you have free time, you could try this too. Even a few hours can help, and the work is more enjoyable than you might expect.
