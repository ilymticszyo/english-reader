Bread is one of the oldest foods in the world, and it is still part of almost every meal in many countries. Most bread is made from only four things: flour, water, salt, and yeast. But how do these simple things become a soft, warm loaf?

The first step is mixing. Flour and water are mixed together to make a thick dough. When water touches the flour, two special proteins join and make a long, stretchy net. This net is called gluten. It is important because it holds the bread together and gives it its shape.

The next step is kneading. The baker pushes and pulls the dough with both hands for about ten minutes. This makes the gluten stronger. Good dough feels smooth and a little sticky, and it springs back when you press it with your finger.

Then comes the most interesting part. The dough is left in a warm place to rise. Yeast is a tiny living thing, and it likes to eat the sugar in the flour. As it eats, it makes a gas called carbon dioxide. The gas fills the gluten net like a balloon, and the dough grows to twice its size. This usually takes one or two hours.

After the dough has risen, the baker shapes it and lets it rest again for a short time. Then the loaf goes into a very hot oven. In the heat, the gas gets bigger and the bread rises a little more. At the same time, the yeast dies, and the outside of the loaf turns brown and hard.

After about thirty minutes, the bread is ready. It is best to wait before cutting it, because the inside is still cooking. When it is cool, you can enjoy the soft inside and the crisp outside.
