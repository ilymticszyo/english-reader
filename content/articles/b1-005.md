Bread is one of the oldest foods in the world, and it is still part of almost every meal in many countries. Most bread is made from only four things: flour, water, salt, and a small amount of yeast. But how do these simple things become a soft, warm loaf?

The first step is mixing. Flour and water are mixed together to make a thick, wet lump. When the water touches the flour, something special happens. A long, stretchy net begins to grow inside the mix. This net is important because it holds the bread together and gives it its shape.

The next step is pushing and pulling the mix with both hands for about ten minutes. This makes the net stronger. Good bread mix feels smooth and a little sticky, and it comes back to its shape when you press it with your finger.

Then comes the most interesting part. The mix is left in a warm place for an hour or two. The yeast is a tiny living thing, and it likes to eat the sugar in the flour. As it eats, it makes a gas. The gas fills the net like a balloon, and the bread mix grows to twice its size. This is why the bread becomes light and soft.

After that, the baker gives the bread its shape and lets it rest again for a short time. Then the loaf goes into a very hot oven. In the heat, the gas gets bigger and the bread rises a little more. At the same time, the yeast dies, and the outside of the loaf turns brown and hard.

After about thirty minutes, the bread is ready. It is best to wait before cutting it, because the inside is still cooking. When it is cool, you can enjoy the soft inside and the crisp outside.
