My grandfather is eighty years old, and until last month, he had never used a smartphone. He lives alone in a small house, and I live in a different city. When my father gave him a phone for his birthday, he was not happy. "What will I do with this thing?" he asked. "It is too complicated."

I decided to teach him. I went to his house for a weekend, and we sat at the kitchen table with the phone between us. The first problem was very simple. He did not know how to turn it on. Then he pressed the screen too hard. "You only need to touch it gently," I explained.

On the first day, we learned how to make a call. He wrote the numbers of his family on a piece of paper and put it next to the phone. By the evening, he could call me without any help.

On the second day, we tried a video call. My grandfather saw my face on the screen, and he did not say anything for a moment. Then he asked, "Can you see me, too?" I said yes, and he started to wave. After that, I called my mother and my cousins, and everybody said hello to him. His eyes were bright.

Then I showed him how to take photos. He wanted to send some to his sister, who lives far away, so I taught him how to send a message.

It was a slow process, and I had to repeat everything many times. But I tried to be patient, because I remembered that he taught me to ride a bicycle in the same way.

Now my grandfather calls me every evening. Sometimes he does not know which button to press, and he phones me to ask. I never mind.

I learned something from this weekend. Technology is easy for young people, but it can be frightening for older ones. With a little time and patience, everyone can learn.
