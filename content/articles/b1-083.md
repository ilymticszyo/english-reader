When I first visited Tokyo, I was afraid of the subway. The map looked like a bowl of colourful noodles. There were so many lines, and every station had a different name. But after a few days, I began to enjoy it.

On the first morning, I stood in front of a ticket machine for ten minutes. I could not decide what to press. A kind woman saw me and showed me how to buy a card. She said, "Put money on it, and then you can use it for everything." I thanked her many times.

The next problem was finding the right platform. Every line has its own colour and a letter. Each station also has a number. So I did not need to read the Japanese names. I just followed the colour and the number on the signs. This made everything simple.

The trains were very clean and always on time. People stood in neat lines on the platform, and nobody pushed. When the doors opened, passengers got off first, and then others got in. Inside the train, it was quiet. Many people read books or looked at their phones. Few people talked, and no one spoke loudly on the phone.

In the morning, the trains were extremely crowded. I was pressed against the door, and I could not move my arms. A man next to me said sorry, though it was not his fault. I smiled and said it was fine.

By the end of the week, I could travel across the city alone. I felt proud. The subway had been the most difficult part of my trip, but it also became my best memory. Now I always tell new visitors, "Do not be afraid. Follow the colours."
